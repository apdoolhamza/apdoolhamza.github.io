My Hometown · Kano State, Nigeria

Kano, the ancient city that never stopped trading.

Behind the old city walls, Kano has been a crossroads of the Sahara for over a thousand years - indigo dye pits, a 500-year-old market, and a skyline of minarets & modern towers side by side.

Why come here

A living trade city, not a museum piece

Kano was already an established city-state on the trans-Saharan trade routes before most European capitals existed. What makes it worth visiting isn't just the history - it's that so much of it is still in daily use: dyers still work the pits, traders still fill Kurmi Market every morning, and the Emir's palace is still occupied.

01

Kofar Mata Dye Pits

The oldest indigo dye pits in Africa, worked continuously since 1498. Watch cloth pulled fresh from the vats and hung to dry in the sun.

02

Kurmi Market

Founded around 1463 by Emir Muhammadu Rumfa, this is one of West Africa's oldest and largest markets - leather, textiles, and spices under one sprawling roof.

03

Gidan Makama Museum

A 15th-century palace turned museum, tracing Kano's history from the Hausa kingdoms through the emirate to today.

The Durbar - Kano at full color

During Eid celebrations, the Emir's cavalry parades through the city in full regalia - the single best time to see Kano's royal traditions up close. It's a UNESCO-recognised intangible heritage practice, shared across Nigeria & several neighbouring countries.
